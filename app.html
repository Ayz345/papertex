You edit the page. PaperTeX writes the LaTeX.

    
Headings, equations, figures and tables are all edited directly on the sheet, and
      the .tex file on the right updates as you go. If you would rather write LaTeX, type it in
      that pane: the page follows.


    
Getting around

    

      /On an empty paragraph, opens the block menu — section, equation, figure, table, list…
      $Inside any text, starts an inline formula right where the cursor is
      Ctrl+KThe command palette: search every block, symbol, structure and command, and see its
        shortcut. In running text \ opens it too (press \ twice for a backslash)
      Ctrl+EnterDrops a display equation below the current block
      EnterNew paragraph · in a list, a new item · in an equation, a new aligned line —
        on an empty last line, back to the text
      TabIndents a list item, moves to the next table cell, steps to the next box of a formula
      ⋮The dots beside a block: change its level, numbering, label, or move it
    


    
Writing formulas — just type the name

    

      intBecomes ∫, with the cursor waiting in the lower limit. Same for
        sum, prod, lim, sqrt, frac, matrix, cases, vec, hat
      EnterDone with this part — on to the next one: lower limit → upper limit → the integrand,
        numerator → denominator → after the fraction, out of an exponent. Space does the same.
        Leave a lower limit empty and it skips straight past the limits. Only on the line itself
        does Enter start a new equation line
      ↑ ↓Go to what is drawn above or below: from the line, ↑ enters the exponent, upper limit or
        numerator beside the cursor and ↓ the subscript, lower limit or denominator; from an exponent,
        ↓ comes back down to the line
      TabSteps through every box in turn, empty ones included. Shift+Tab goes back
      ^From inside a lower limit, jumps up to the upper one — _ jumps back down
      alphaBecomes α — every Greek letter, plus inf → ∞, partial → ∂,
        nabla → ∇, in → ∈, cdot → ⋅
      sinBecomes an upright function name, as \sin should be
      ->Becomes →  ·  <= ≤  ·  >= ≥  ·  != ≠  ·  +- ±
      BackspaceStraight after one of those puts the plain letters back
      /Fraction — the term just before it becomes the numerator: dy/dt, (a+b)/2,
        and in (T_1-T_0)x/L only the x. The Fraction button and frac give an empty one to fill in.
        For a plain slash, as in k/m, type / twice (or Backspace straight after it)
      ^ Superscript  ·  _ subscript  ·  type both for a stacked pair.
        ü also gives a superscript, for keyboards where ^ is a dead key
      x1A digit straight after a letter becomes an index: x1 → x₁, a12 → a₁₂, alpha3 → α₃.
        Only digits go on into it — x1+x2 and x1, x2 come out as you mean them, and
        x1^2 gives x₁². Backspace straight after puts the plain digit back
      (Brackets that grow with their contents — | gives absolute value
      →Out of an index, exponent, numerator or root, on along the line (Space too).
        The part you are typing in is shaded
      matrixTyping matrix or cases asks how big, on a small grid. Tab goes on to the next
        cell. Double-click a matrix to edit it large, with buttons for rows, columns and brackets
      . , ;Typed at the end of an exponent, index or root, they go after it: x^2. is x² followed by a full stop
      HomeThe start of the whole formula — in front of a matrix or cases brace too. End its end
      Ctrl+BBold letters, for vectors — or type bold
      \The long way round, if you know LaTeX: it is read as LaTeX reads it — \frac12, \left( … \right),
        \begin{cases} … & … \\ … \end{cases}, \, for a thin space. A command PaperTeX does not know is shown
        in red and written to the LaTeX as typed
      EscLeaves the formula: back to the text, or on to a new line below an equation
    


    
Good to know

    

      SelectHighlighting text pops up bold, italic, code, cross-reference and “make this a formula”
      Ctrl+ V an image anywhere on the page drops in a figure — dragging one in works too
      PasteLaTeX pasted onto the page is read, not pasted as source: a sentence goes in at the cursor
        with its formulas and citations, sections and equations come in as blocks, a whole document can open on its own.
        Pasted into a formula, LaTeX becomes editable maths
      ReferencesTo refer to an equation, table, figure or section, or to cite a work:
        Ctrl+K → “reference” or “cite”, or the ↪ button on selected text.
        Numbers keep up as things move
      FootnoteCtrl+K → “footnote” puts one at the cursor; click its number to change it
      ProofsCtrl+Enter in a theorem or proof puts the equation inside it, before the □
      Ctrl+SDownloads the .tex — images come with it, keep them in the same folder
      Ctrl+OOpens a .tex file — or a whole Overleaf project: its “Download source” .zip, or the
        .tex with its .bib and images picked together. Sections, equations, references and images become editable;
        anything PaperTeX does not edit on the page stays as LaTeX code, still in the PDF
      LaTeX paneType in the LaTeX on the right; when you pause, the page follows. A half-typed
        \begin{…} waits for its \end. Esc gives the keys back to the page
      Your keysEvery command, block and symbol can have a shortcut of your own, and so can any
        LaTeX snippet — 
      CompileTypesets it with real LaTeX in your browser — nothing is uploaded. First run downloads the engine
