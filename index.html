1 Introduction

For a quantity x with mean ⟨x⟩, the spread is σ2 = ⟨x2⟩ − ⟨x⟩2. The integral of a Gaussian over the whole real line has a closed form,

∫−∞∞  e−x² dx = √π(1)

which we prove in Section 2. For two quantities the spreads form a matrix,

Σ = σx2ρσxσyρσxσyσy2(2)
\begin{equation}
  \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-x^{2}}\,dx = \sqrt{\pi}
  \label{eq:gauss}
\end{equation}
\Sigma = \begin{pmatrix}
  \sigma_x^2 & \rho\sigma_x\sigma_y \\
  \rho\sigma_x\sigma_y & \sigma_y^2
\end{pmatrix}

2 Proof

Write I for the integral in (1). Squaring it gives a double integral over the plane, which polar coordinates make elementary:

I2 = ∫02π ∫0∞ e−r² r dr dθ = π(3)

Hence I = √π, as claimed. ∎
